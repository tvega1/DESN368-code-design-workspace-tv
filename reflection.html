The Threshold

What was the specific moment this quarter where "code" clicked for you?

When I realized how easy a hover is, code suddenly made sense to me. I've always seen animated buttons on websites and figured they needed complicated code or specific skills. It was like a lightbulb turning on when I realized that it only required changing a property when the user interacted with the element. Something that appeared "advanced" at first turned out to be just a few CSS lines that I was still unfamiliar with. My approach to coding changed after that; instead of being afraid of what I didn't know, I became enthusiastic about the numerous "simple but powerful" tools that were just waiting for me to find them.

The Pit

What was the hardest assignment? How did you get yourself out of the "pit of despair"?

My product landing page was the hardest assignment, especially when my CSS wouldn't load. No matter how many times I refreshed the page, nothing changed, even though I thought I had done everything right. I had no idea whether the problem was in my HTML, CSS, my browser, or my wifi, so it was a real pit of misery. Slowing down to check the basics—file structure, naming, and paths—plus a little help from ChatGPT, was what finally pulled me out of the mess. It was both a comfort and a lesson when I discovered the issue—a little file name mismatch—because code only cares about accuracy, not intentions.

The Pivot

Look at your original "Moodz and Vibes." How does your final portfolio compare? Did you stick to the plan or pivot? Why?

My Figma mood board began with a warm, witchy, and cozy look, and I'm pleased to see how well my finished portfolio captures that idea. I stuck with dark, warm colors, text that feels like it belongs in a candlelit library, and natural textures that fit the theme. My almost Halloween theme. Rather than giving up on my style in favor of something more "techy" or minimalist, I tried to translate my aesthetic into simple and practical UI decisions. The mood never changed, but I fine-tuned things like contrast and spacing so the design stayed just as cozy while still being easy to read on any screen. Since I only made it practical rather than altering the tone, this felt like a legitimate design choice rather than a compromise.

The Process

What would you change about how you approached this course?

If I could redo the course, I would approach the assignments more carefully. I love trying things, but sometimes I jump straight into applying new techniques to my main project before understanding them fully. I had made too many changes at once, making it more difficult to identify the problem when something broke. I've discovered that little test files are useful since they allow me to experiment safely and understand the reasoning before applying it to a larger project. I’d continue to explore, but with more intention, turning experiments into tools for learning rather than quick solutions.

The Future

How do you see this hybrid skillset (Design + Code) impacting your future practice?

As someone who wants to be a designer, learning how to code gives me a huge advantage. I can build with actual limits in mind rather than simply wishful thinking with Figma because I now understand HTML and CSS. I can make designs that are both creative and practical, work better with developers, and build things myself instead of guessing what’s possible. Not only does learning to code improve my computer science skills, but it also makes me a more capable designer who can translate ideas into actual experiences.
